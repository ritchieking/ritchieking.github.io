Paid sharing, start to finish

2020–2026

Netflix’s paid-sharing initiative moved from an idea to a quasi-experiment to a global launch. I gave each phase its own focused tool and did the early last-mile data work myself.

  • A scrollytelling tool explaining our sharing detection methodology, with diagrams that update as you read.
  • Test-market dashboards for the two competing approaches to monetizing sharing, with matching hero charts so leadership could compare them side by side and choose a winner.
  • The global launch dashboard: a catalog of ~40 agreed metric definitions, an executive snapshot, a drill-down explorer, and annotated event timelines. Hundreds of colleagues used it, from product and marketing to legal and the executive office.
  • UI patterns other teams picked up from this work: a metric index, full-screen zoomable charts, custom market groupings, and per-chart permalinks.

Business tools for leadership

2018–2026

The apps growth and finance leaders used to make decisions about pricing, revenue, payments, and membership.

  • Co-developed the company’s main business dashboard. I added guided tours, a semi-automated headline briefing, and pricing views. It was used by thousands of colleagues.
  • A price-change efficiency view, used in Netflix’s price-change reviews, that assesses the success of every price change and breaks down its impact on revenue. I also built a narrative graphic to explain the underlying methodology.
  • A membership-lifecycle app, built based on a perceived need, not a direct request from stakeholders. I developed novel visualizations, pioneered a new approach to working with agents, and owned part of the underlying data model.
  • A rebuilt payments dashboard and, earlier, the plan-pricing and quarterly revenue apps.

Growing the team

2024–2025

Designed the hiring process for data viz engineers on my team: three screens, including live coding and a chart-editing test, then a four-part panel.

  • Mentored both of our new hires through pair programming and code review. Within two years, each was leading a flagship launch.
  • Took over roadmapping for the team’s viz portfolio.
  • Other teams adopted it, along with the four-week onboarding plan I wrote.

Enabling others

2025–2026

Rebuilt Netflix’s workforce-analytics app using building blocks an analytics engineer with little front-end experience could extend. I then coached him through code review until he owned it.

  • Built the sensitive pieces myself: an org-hierarchy picker and small-group privacy suppression.
  • He went on to ship v2 on his own. The app serves every manager of 50+ people and all of HR.

Viz engineering for the agent era

2025–2026

On Netflix’s generative-AI task force for data, I worked on how viz engineers should build data products with the help of agents.

  • Benchmarked agents recreating ~300 charts using Netflix’s internal charting library, with an eval that pinpointed where models struggled. The findings fed the agent skills published for the wider org.
  • Built a remote agent orchestration system based on a “brain and hands” model, with bootstrap docs so others could create their own version.
  • Built a gallery where people and agents publish and reuse UI components.
  • Gave talks on labs-based product development: prototyping several options side by side, then refining, freezing, or killing each one.
